From the Sideline to the Baseline: Shout or Shut Up?

At my daughter’s soccer games, everyone seems to have something to say except the children: coaches shout instructions, and parents call from the sidelines. At a junior tennis tournament, most adults suddenly go quiet, leaving the children to call their own lines, handle disputes, and solve problems for themselves—even when that means watching them make mistakes we are not allowed to fix. These sports don’t just teach children how to play; they teach adults how to parent.

Tennis vs. Pickleball: Friend or Foe?

Tennis and pickleball should be natural allies: they share courts, coaches, racquets (or paddles), and plenty of players who happily cross between the two. Instead, their relationship can feel more like a family feud, complete with territorial disputes, complaints about noise, arguments over technique and footwork, and plenty players who quietly confess their crossing over to the enemy. When a new sport moves into an old sport’s territory, do they learn to share—or start drawing battle lines?.

Love on the Line

My realtor once told me that if you want to test a marriage, renovate a house together. But perhaps there is a cheaper experiment: enter a mixed-doubles tennis league. For couples willing to spend an entire season sharing a court, every missed volley, questionable strategy, and unsolicited suggestion with a badly timed “You should have…” becomes a small test of whether love can survive competitive sports.
